How to write a tender response that actually scores

Public sector bids are scored, not read for pleasure. An evaluator has a marking grid, a word limit, and a dozen other bids to get through. Winning is mostly about making it easy for them to give you marks.

Start from the scoring, not the question

Every quality question has a weighting and usually a rubric (what earns a top mark vs a middling one). Read that first. If "excellent" requires specific evidence and a worked example, give exactly that. Writing beautifully to the wrong target scores poorly.

Structure every answer the same way

A reliable pattern per question:

  1. Direct answer in the first sentence — state what you do/will do.
  2. How — your method, briefly, in their terms.
  3. Evidence — a real example, with a number or outcome ("reduced downtime by 30%", "delivered 14 similar contracts").
  4. Relevance — one line tying it back to this buyer's need.

Write for a tired evaluator

  • Short paragraphs, clear subheadings, and the key point first.
  • Use their language and the contract's own terms.
  • Respect the word/character limit — over-length answers get truncated or penalised.
  • No marketing fluff: "passionate", "world-class" and "synergy" earn zero marks.

Social value is real marks now

Scottish and UK public buyers weight community benefit / social value — local employment, training, carbon reduction, supporting other small firms. These are often 10–20% of the score and small businesses frequently leave them blank. Offer something specific and deliverable, not aspirational.

Price to win, not to impress

Price is scored on a formula, usually relative to the lowest bid. Understand the formula before you set your number. The cheapest bid rarely needs to win if quality is weighted heavily — but an unrealistically high price loses regardless of quality.

Before you submit

  • Answer every question (a blank scores zero and can fail you).
  • Attach every mandatory document (insurance, certs, signed forms).
  • Re-read against the rubric: would a stranger give you the top mark on the evidence here?
  • Submit a day early — portals are slow at the deadline and late is excluded.

Always request a debrief afterwards, win or lose. The scores and comments tell you precisely where to improve — it's the fastest way to lift your hit rate on the next bid.
