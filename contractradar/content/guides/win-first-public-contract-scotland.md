# How to win your first public sector contract in Scotland
> A practical, jargon-free guide for small businesses bidding for Scottish public contracts for the first time.

Scotland's public sector spends billions a year with external suppliers, and a large share is legally required to be advertised openly. If you run a small business, a surprising amount of it is winnable — but the process has its own language and rhythm. Here's how to start without wasting weeks.

## 1. Get registered where the work is advertised
Most Scottish public contracts are posted on **Public Contracts Scotland (PCS)**. Create a free supplier account at publiccontractsscotland.gov.uk. Lower-value work is often issued as a **Quick Quote** — invited, faster, and well suited to smaller firms — so complete your PCS profile and category codes properly, because that is how buyers find you for those.

You will also need a **Unique Entity / supplier identifier** and, for many tenders, to be registered on the relevant supplier systems the notice names. Do this before you find a live opportunity, not during the bid window.

## 2. Understand the thresholds
Roughly: lower-value requirements are quoted quickly and informally; regulated procurements above the Scottish thresholds (currently tens of thousands for goods and services, higher for works) must be advertised and follow a formal process. The threshold matters because it tells you how much process to expect — a £15k quick quote is a different effort to a £2m framework.

## 3. Pick winnable contracts, not exciting ones
The most common mistake is chasing contracts that are too big or outside your core. For your first win, filter hard:
- **In your sector** (match the CPV code to what you actually do).
- **In your region** or genuinely deliverable remotely.
- **A value you can resource** without betting the company.
- **Enough time left** to write a proper response (walk away from a 3-day deadline).

This is exactly the filtering BidBeacon automates — but you can do it by hand on PCS to start.

## 4. Read the whole notice before you commit
Download the full tender pack. Check: the deadline, the scoring weightings (price vs quality), mandatory requirements (insurance levels, certifications), and whether it's a one-off or a framework. If you can't meet a mandatory requirement, stop now — no quality of writing recovers a failed pass/fail gate.

## 5. Build a reusable bid library
Most tenders ask the same things: company background, relevant experience, approach/method, social value, health & safety, quality and environmental policies. Write good answers once, keep them in a folder, and tailor them each time. Your second bid takes a third of the effort of your first.

## 6. Answer the question they asked
Score comes from evidence against the published criteria, not enthusiasm. For each question: answer it directly, give a specific example with a number or outcome, and map your answer to what they said they'd score. Avoid generic claims ("we are passionate about quality") with no evidence behind them.

## 7. Submit early
Portals get slow near deadlines and late bids are simply excluded — no appeal. Aim to submit the day before.

---

**The honest part:** your first bid may not win, and that's normal. Debrief feedback is free and legally available — always request it, because it tells you exactly where you lost marks. Treat the first two or three bids as paid-for market research, and your hit rate climbs fast once your bid library matures.
