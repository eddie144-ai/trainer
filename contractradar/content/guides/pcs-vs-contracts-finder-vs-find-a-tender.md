# PCS vs Contracts Finder vs Find a Tender: which should you watch?
> The three main UK public contract portals explained, and which ones matter if you're a Scottish business.

If you're hunting public sector work from Scotland, three official portals cover most of it. They overlap, they each miss things, and watching only one is the single most common way businesses never see contracts they could have won.

## Public Contracts Scotland (PCS)
**The primary source for Scotland.** Scottish law requires public bodies to advertise regulated procurement here, and it also carries lower-value **Quick Quotes** that are ideal for small firms. If you do one thing, register on PCS and keep your profile current. Most Scottish councils, NHS boards, universities and the Scottish Government post here.

## Contracts Finder
**UK-wide, but England-weighted.** Run by the UK government, it carries contracts (generally above £12,000) mostly from English and reserved/UK-wide bodies. A Scottish business will find some relevant UK-wide opportunities here — but far fewer purely Scottish ones than on PCS. Worth watching as a secondary source, not your main one.

## Find a Tender (FTS)
**High-value only.** FTS replaced the EU's OJEU for higher-value UK contracts (typically above ~£139k for goods/services, much higher for works). If you bid for larger contracts or frameworks, watch it; if your sweet spot is sub-£100k work, it will mostly be noise.

## The practical takeaway
- **Small Scottish business, smaller contracts:** PCS is essential; the others are optional extras.
- **Growing, bidding UK-wide or on frameworks:** watch all three.
- **The gap everyone hits:** each portal has its own search, its own alerts, and no cross-portal view — so opportunities fall between them, and the alerts are keyword-crude (you drown in irrelevant notices or miss relevant ones).

That cross-portal, relevance-ranked view is exactly the gap BidBeacon fills: it pulls the opportunity feeds together, scores each against your business, and emails you only the ones that fit. But the portals are free and public — this guide is here so you can work them directly if you'd rather.
